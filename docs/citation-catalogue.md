# Citation catalogue: the battery's life after its first passport

This catalogue quotes every provision that the proposal [CX-0160-4 Battery Passport after the First Passport](proposal/CX-0160-4_Battery_passport_after_the_first_passport.md) and the [methodology](methodology.md) cite on the battery's life after its first passport. Each quote is verbatim, holds at most 40 words and names its locator.

- A quote of a text that this repository stores in `sources/` names the path of the stored file and the line of the quote.
- A quote of a text that this repository cites and does not store names the text, its version and its section. The file `sources/README.md` lists those texts and the reason for each one.
- The words "stored text" in this catalogue mean a text that the author holds and has read. The file `sources/README.md` states which of those texts this repository redistributes.
- The numbers 265 and 266 are not used: two citations of a document of the owner left the catalogue on 2026-11-16, and every other citation keeps its number.
- The owner of this repository is the author of the proposal. The decisions that the catalogue names are records of `requirements/SYS.1-Requirements_Elicitation/decisions.json`.

## 1. The result

- **287 citations from four groups of texts are verified.** The 287 citations hold 85 quotes from Regulation (EU) 2023/1542, 58 quotes from DIN DKE SPEC 99100, 70 quotes from the secondary European Union (EU) acts and 74 quotes from the Catena-X texts, the texts of the Industrial Digital Twin Association (IDTA) and the Eclipse Tractus-X texts. Section 6 states how the quotes were checked.
- **The owner's recycling premise is partly supported** (section 2). The recycler reports the recycling efficiency and the recovered materials to the competent authority, not to a manufacturer (Art. 75(5)). The operator that places a battery on the market again after preparation for re-use, preparation for repurposing, repurposing or remanufacturing becomes the producer of that battery (Art. 56(2)). The original producer therefore keeps no reporting duty for that battery. A notice to the original manufacturer after recycling is a voluntary data service, which no stored text requires.
- **CX-0160 1.1.0 has no rule for the battery's life after its first passport.** The base document mentions second life and recycling only in its introduction and in its scope. CX-0160 1.1.0 carries the battery status through the mandatory element LifeCycleStage of the IDTA digital nameplate 1.0.0, whose description states when a new passport is issued, but CX-0160 1.1.0 has no rule for issuing the new passport, no link between passports, no end of a passport and no access tiers (section 3). CX-0160 1.1.0 already requires the Digital Twin Event notification of CX-0151 (sections 4.2 and 5.1.4), with the event CreateSubmodel only.
- **The Tractus-X models can link one predecessor only.** The generic digital product passport model 7.0.0 holds one optional `predecessor` identifier, and the remanufacturing certificate 3.0.0 holds one optional `catenaXIdPreviousLife`. Art. 77(7) of Regulation (EU) 2023/1542 links a new passport to "the battery passport or passports of the original battery or batteries".
- **No stored text defines a repaired status.** DIN DKE SPEC 99100 §6.1.3.7 g) records a repair under the status "re-used", and the product status values of the generic digital product passport models 5.0.0 and 7.0.0 have no value for a repair. CX-0131 Circularity Core 1.1.1 names a repair certificate (version 1.0.0, not stored) on the twin of the same part instance, and recommends that the company performing a reuse, repair, remanufacturing, refurbishment or material recycling operation issues the certificate of that operation.
- **The open readings reduced to four new decisions** (section 4). Seven earlier decisions answer the other readings: Q-021 (the reuse action that creates a new passport), Q-022 (whether waste status and recycling are recorded as separate events), Q-023 (whether a new passport may link to several original passports), Q-026 (whether a new passport gets the new manufacturer's battery model), Q-028 (the passport state of a remanufacturing donor battery), Q-969 (whether a new passport keeps the battery's unique identifier) and Q-998 (whether the dataset keeps the link to the predecessor passport after the CX-Neptune go-live). The four new decisions are Q-1007 (which producer takes over the passport of a second-life battery that becomes waste), Q-1008 (whether the original passport records a link to its successor passport), Q-1009 (whether a new passport after a second-life operation is a new registration in the EU registry) and Q-1010 (who authorises the wider sharing of a second-life battery's passport data).
- **Three free texts that the readings need are stored in `sources/`** (section 5): Directive 2008/98/EC on waste, the generic digital product passport model 5.0.0 and the recycling strategy certificate 2.0.0. Model 5.0.0 gives BatteryPass 6.1.0 the status values "original", "repurposed", "re-used", "remanufactured" and "waste", with no value for a repair, and one `predecessor` identifier only.

## 2. The premise check: who learns the recycling result

The owner's premise of 2026-11-05 reads: the manufacturers and economic operators must report statistics on the recycling of their batteries, so they must receive notifications and data when their batteries are recycled, even after the batteries have been re-used or remanufactured. Decision Q-1006 (how the producers of a battery learn its recycling result after re-use or remanufacturing) rests on this check.

| Question | Answer of the stored texts | Locators |
|---|---|---|
| Who reports the recycling efficiency and the recovered materials? | The waste management operators carrying out treatment and the recyclers report to the competent authority of the Member State where the treatment takes place. Where several facilities recycle, the first recycler collects and reports the information. | Regulation (EU) 2023/1542 Art. 75(5) first and second subparagraphs, Art. 75(5)(c); Delegated Regulation (EU) 2025/606 Annex section 5 point 1 and section 10 point 3 |
| Where does that information go next? | The competent authority of the Member State of treatment passes the information to the competent authority of the Member State of collection. The Member States publish aggregated values. | Art. 75(5) third subparagraph; Art. 76(1)(d); Delegated Regulation (EU) 2025/606 Annex section 10 point 5 |
| What do producers report? | The producers, or their producer responsibility organisations, report the amounts placed on the market, collected and delivered to treatment, preparation for re-use or preparation for repurposing. The producers do not report the recycling efficiency. | Art. 75(1) and 75(2) |
| Does a producer see the recycling result of its own batteries? | A producer responsibility organisation publishes each year the recycling efficiencies and the levels of recovery of materials "achieved by the producers which appointed" the organisation. The organisation therefore needs those values per producer from the recyclers it contracts. | Art. 57(5) |
| Who is the producer after preparation for re-use, preparation for repurposing, repurposing or remanufacturing? | The economic operator that first makes the resulting battery available on the market in a Member State is the producer of that battery and has the extended producer responsibility. The same operator is a manufacturer for the purposes of the Regulation. | Art. 56(2); Art. 38(11); Recital 16 |
| Does the original producer keep a duty for the battery's later life? | The original producer "should not bear the additional costs" of the waste management of the battery's later life. A cost sharing mechanism between the original and the later producers is optional. | Recital 102; Art. 56(5) first and second subparagraphs |
| Does a plain re-use change the producer? | A battery that is only re-used is considered already placed on the market, so the original producer keeps its extended producer responsibility for that battery. | Recital 16; Art. 56(1) |
| Does any text require a notice to the original manufacturer after recycling? | No stored text requires such a notice. Art. 77(8) ends the passport after recycling. The draft Art. 77(9) act and Delegated Regulation (EU) 2025/606 create no notification duty. CX-0151 leaves the content of a notification to each use case. | Art. 77(8); draft Art. 77(9) act recital 2; CX-0151 1.1.0 |

**Verdict: partly supported.** The premise holds for the producer of the battery that is recycled: that producer, or its producer responsibility organisation, needs the recycling efficiency and the recovery of materials achieved for its batteries (Art. 57(5)). After preparation for re-use, preparation for repurposing, repurposing or remanufacturing, that producer is the second-life operator, not the original manufacturer (Art. 56(2)). The premise also holds for the original manufacturer of a battery that is only re-used, because the original manufacturer stays the producer of that battery (Recital 16). The premise fails for the original manufacturer of a battery that another operator has placed on the market again.

**Consequence for decision Q-1006.** The recycler must reach one party by regulation: the producer of the passport that recycling ends, or the producer's responsibility organisation. That party is named in the final passport itself, so the recycler needs no walk along the predecessor links for the regulatory duty. A notice to the original manufacturers along the predecessor links is a voluntary service: the original manufacturer could use the notice for a cost sharing mechanism (Art. 56(5)) or for its own design feedback. The use cases of the [methodology](methodology.md) carry the system requirements of both flows, and the owner answered decision Q-1006 on 2026-11-09.

## 3. What no stored text defines

The Catena-X and Tractus-X texts of the release that binds now (CX-Titan) and of the preview (CX-Neptune) leave these points open. The proposal CX-0160-4 addresses each point.

1. CX-0160 1.1.0 has no rule for a new passport after preparation for re-use, preparation for repurposing, repurposing or remanufacturing.
2. CX-0160 1.1.0 carries the battery status in the mandatory element LifeCycleStage of the IDTA digital nameplate 1.0.0 (IDTA-02035-1), with the five values of Annex XIII point 4(c), and states no rule for the change of the status. BatteryPass 6.1.0 takes its status from the generic digital product passport model 5.0.0 (the property `state`, serialised as "status", with the values original, repurposed, re-used, remanufactured and waste), and model 7.0.0 renames the property `productStatus`.
3. CX-0160 1.1.0 mentions second life and recycling only in its introduction and in its scope. The use case document CX-0160-3, section 5.2, names the case where the economic operator changes after a status change of the battery, without a rule.
4. CX-0127 2.0.2 (CX-Titan) and CX-0127 3.0.0 (CX-Neptune) have no rule for a new digital twin, a new `partInstanceId` or a new `catenaXId` after a second-life operation.
5. No stored Catena-X model links a new passport to several predecessor passports.
6. No stored Catena-X text defines the end of a passport after recycling (Art. 77(8)).
7. No stored Catena-X text defines a repaired battery status or a repair history entry of the passport. CX-0131 Circularity Core 1.1.1 (the same text in CX-Titan and CX-Neptune) names a repair certificate submodel `repairCrt_{{PartInstanceId}}` on the twin of the repaired part instance, whose model version 1.0.0 the author does not hold. The Industry Core KIT marks the bill of materials as maintained ("AsMaintained") as not implemented.
8. No stored Catena-X text defines a notice from a recycler or a second-life operator to a producer. CX-0160 1.1.0 requires the Digital Twin Event notification of CX-0151 for a new submodel (sections 4.2 and 5.1.4), and names no event for a new passport, a waste status or the end of a passport. CX-0151 1.1.0 leaves the notification content to each use case, the digital twin event API knows only the creation, update and deletion of a submodel, and the traceability notification API 2.0.0 covers only quality investigations and quality alerts.
9. CX-0151 1.1.0 does not say how a sender finds the connector of the receiver. Only the Eco Pass KIT describes the BPN Discovery Service and the connector discovery service, and a KIT is guidance, not a standard.
10. CX-0160 1.1.0 has no access tiers. CX-0160 registers one usage purpose, `cx.circular.dpp:1`, with no rule for public, legitimate-interest or authority access.

DIN DKE SPEC 99100 requires a new passport (§5.2.1 d) and §6.1.3.7 e)) but defines no link attribute and no end of the passport after recycling. Recovered materials and recycling efficiency appear only in Annex XII of Regulation (EU) 2023/1542, not in DIN DKE SPEC 99100.

## 6. How the catalogue was checked

Four collections read the four groups of texts in parallel. On 2026-11-05 a verification script read each quoted file of the author and found every one of the 287 quotes on its stated line, with at most 40 words each. The script found no quote on another line and rejected none.

On 2026-11-16 a second script searched each quote of a text stored in `sources/` in the stored file of this repository. The second script found each of the 160 quotes on its stated line. The other 127 quotes belong to texts that this repository cites and does not store, so this repository cannot repeat the check of those quotes.

The readings in the tables below are the readings of the collections. The owner reviews the readings, and the use cases of the [methodology](methodology.md) rest only on the provisions whose reading the owner accepts.
